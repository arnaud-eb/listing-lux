## Mandatory disclosures — energy class (CPE / Energiepass) findings

**Legal frame.** Luxembourg law has required an energy performance certificate (Certificat de Performance Énergétique — CPE / Energiepass) for the sale, rental, renovation, extension, or construction of a residential building since 2007. Since 1 January 2010, landlords are required to display the EPC in property advertisements. Since 1 July 2012, the energy performance class **and** the thermal insulation class must appear in any sale/rental advertisement published in commercial media. The CPE is rated A (best) → I (worst) and is valid 10 years. Source: [Guichet.lu — Requesting an Energiepass](https://guichet.public.lu/en/citoyens/logement/acquisition/performances-energie/demande-passeport-energetique.html); [PropertyWeb by CBRE — EPC Luxembourg](https://propertyweb.lu/en/insights/the-energy-performance-certificate-epc-in-luxembourg/385); [JustArrived.lu — CPE Passeport Énergétique](https://www.justarrived.lu/en/logement-luxembourg/cpe-passeport-energetique/).

**Observed UX implementation across the four sources:**
- **athome.lu** has dedicated "Energy class" and "Thermal insulation class" fields in the structured-characteristics table on every detail page. The fields appear mid-page under a "Characteristics → Energy / heating" section, **not** in the hero or near the price. Observed values range from A ([id-8276141](https://www.athome.lu/en/buy/apartment/luxembourg/id-8276141.html)) through F ([id-9029592](https://www.athome.lu/en/buy/apartment/luxembourg/id-9029592.html)) to H/I ([id-8333639](https://www.athome.lu/en/buy/house/luxembourg/id-8333639.html)). Fields are frequently rendered as visibly **blank** — the listing UI lets agents publish without filling them ([id-7896685](https://www.athome.lu/en/buy/house/luxembourg/id-7896685.html), [id-8564027](https://www.athome.lu/en/buy/house/hesperange/id-8564027.html), [id-8802095](https://www.athome.lu/en/buy/apartment/luxembourg/id-8802095.html) all show blank energy fields, including a €4.5M and €6.49M property). For new builds (2025) the fields are often blank under the rationale that the certificate is post-construction.
- **immotop.lu** detail pages were 403-blocked from WebFetch; the energy-class field was not surfaced in the listing-card snippets observed. Because direct fetching failed, no observation can be made on its prominence.
- **Engel & Völkers Luxembourg** displays both energy class and thermal class in a structured "Energy efficiency information" section, including the certificate type ("Demand certificate"). All 5 detail pages inspected had the energy class populated (B, D, E twice, blank-mid for newer apartments). E&V additionally exposes heating subtype and ventilation type — beyond the legal minimum.
- **FARE** displays an "Energy Passport" rating in the characteristics table and, distinctively, the [Echternach](https://fare.lu/property/house-in-echternach-86722530/) listing surfaces it in the **opening description paragraph** as "Energy Passport A / A+" — the only observed instance where the rating leaves the structured-fields zone and enters the marketing prose. Older FARE properties ([Soleuvre 1967](https://fare.lu/property/house-in-soleuvre-86819998/), [Limpertsberg 1988](https://fare.lu/property/apartment-in-luxembourg-85578840)) show the field as "not provided" / "not listed".

**Observation on enforcement-by-UX.** Across the audit, the visible UX on athome.lu, FARE, and (where surfaced) E&V allows agents to publish a listing with empty energy/thermal fields. The fields are **present** (consistent with the post-2012 advertising requirement) but **not enforced** (the same listing can ship with blanks). The €6,490,000 Hesperange villa and the €4,500,000 Belair townhouse — both well above the legal threshold — are published with blank energy class.
